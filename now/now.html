this month i made a small feature in an australian financial review article about working from home from the cricket. there were only 35 tickets available for what was an otherwise quiet second day of the sheffield shield, and from all appearances the other attendees did seem to get some work done. more distracting then the slow game of cricket that occurred below me as i drank the coffee that came with my ticket were the journalists of all mastheads who descended on the place from all angles.

the rmit students who organised the program with cricket victoria told me they were shocked by how much media attention their group project got (there was also tea and biscuits, which weren’t mentioned in any article i could find). and from what I heard later, the journalist and photographer from the AFR were unable to enjoy their working from home from the cricket experience, reportedly being asked to leave since they hadn't purchased a ticket.

back in august i promised to tend to the parts of the backyard i hadn’t visited yet. i am pleased to report that the lawn has been mown. it was an extremely hot day, and i was deeply involved in the process, in that i watched dom do it and then swept the enormous amount of grass into the green bin, which i think is equally as hard and just as tedious of a task.
